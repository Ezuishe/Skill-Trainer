Method

How the programs are built

A training plan is a set of claims: that these hours produce that capability, that this drill trains that weakness, that you will know when you have arrived. Those claims should be inspectable. This page states them.

01

What the planner does

You supply four numbers: a horizon in weeks, weekly practice hours, sessions per week, and your honest starting level. The planner multiplies the first two into a total hour budget, credits an allowance for prior experience, and compares the result against four thresholds held for each discipline — functional, working competence, professional, elite.

It then states which threshold the budget reaches. If that is lower than the level you might have hoped for, it says so before you begin, and quantifies the three ways out: extend the horizon, raise the intensity, or narrow the scope. This is the entire point of the instrument. A plan that promises mastery in four weeks is not ambitious, it is simply wrong, and it fails in week five when reality arrives.

Where the budget is too small to cover a discipline properly, whole pillars are cut rather than thinned. Eighty hours spread across five pillars produces five superficial acquaintances; eighty hours on two produces two usable competences. The program tells you exactly what was excluded.

02

Where the hour estimates come from

They are informed approximations, and they should be read as approximations. They are anchored on three things: published work on skill acquisition and expertise, the typical structure of professional training and apprenticeship in each field, and the observable time it takes a motivated adult to reach a level where other people will pay for the work.

They are not measurements of you. Individual variation is large, prior adjacent experience moves the numbers substantially, and the quality of feedback available to you probably matters more than the raw hour count. Treat the thresholds as a way to check ambition against arithmetic, not as a schedule your biology has agreed to.

The frequently quoted "10,000 hours" figure is a misreading of the research it comes from. That work described the accumulated practice of elite violinists — a specific population in a specific field with continuous expert coaching — not a universal requirement. Time to competence varies enormously by domain, and the first useful level in most fields arrives far earlier than the popular number suggests.
03

The training principles

Deliberate practice, not exposure

Practice that improves performance is effortful, targeted at a specific weakness, and followed by feedback. Repeating what you can already do is maintenance. Every session in a program names a target and a difficulty band.

Retrieval over review

Recalling material from memory produces durable learning; rereading produces the feeling of learning. Every session opens with closed-book recall of the previous one, which is the cheapest large gain available in the whole method.

Spacing and interleaving

Sessions of the same type are separated rather than blocked. Interleaved practice feels worse during training and performs better afterwards — one of the most robust and most counter-intuitive findings in the literature.

Production over consumption

The proportion of acquisition sessions falls across the program while production rises. Consuming material about a skill is the most comfortable substitute for practising it, and the most common way programs quietly fail.

Gates, not calendars

Each phase ends with criteria another person could verify. Advancing on time served rather than evidence is how people arrive at week twelve with a certificate and no capability.

Scheduled consolidation

Programs of ten weeks or more include reduced-load weeks. Recovery taken deliberately, before it is needed, prevents the abandonment that typically arrives somewhere around week seven.

04

What this cannot do

It cannot guarantee that you will be successful. Skill is one input into outcomes, and it shares the stage with circumstance, timing, capital, health, obligations and luck. Anyone selling a direct line from a training program to a particular life is selling the part they cannot deliver.

It cannot supply feedback. This is the largest gap between a plan and a coach, and it is why each discipline names an arena — the place where real signal about your work exists. A program run entirely in private will plateau, and you will not notice when it does.

It cannot make you do the work, and it does not try to motivate you. There are no streak animations or congratulatory noises here, because those train enthusiasm for the interface rather than for the skill. What it offers is a schedule specific enough that showing up requires no decisions.

Finally, it is a general instrument. It does not know your circumstances, your existing obligations or your health. Material touching money and physical training is general education, not professional advice — for decisions that depend on your particular situation, consult someone qualified who can see it.

05

Data and privacy

There is no account, no server and no analytics. Your program, your progress, your logs and your dispatch preferences are stored in this browser’s local storage and go nowhere else. Clearing your browser data deletes them; using a different device shows you nothing.

This is a deliberate constraint rather than a missing feature. It also means the export buttons matter: download the Markdown or the calendar file if the program is something you intend to keep.
