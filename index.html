What this does

No promises

Nobody can guarantee you a skill, and a site that says it can is selling you something. What a plan can do is put your hours against a real syllabus and make it harder to lie to yourself about how it is going.

Sized to your hours

Four weeks at five hours a week is twenty hours. That is a real amount of training and it will not make you a professional at anything. The planner works out what your number reaches and tells you before you commit to it.

Drills, not a reading list

Every phase gives you named drills with a dose, the protocol, and the mistake most people make. Reading is capped at a quarter of your time, because it is the easiest way to feel like you are improving while you are not.

01

The disciplines

Thirteen skills chosen because they compound. Each one turns into money, freedom or better judgement, and keeps paying long after the plan is finished. Pick one. Running two at once mostly means going slowly at both.

The hour figures mean focused practice with some kind of feedback. They do not mean time spent near the subject with a podcast on.

02

Build your plan

Be honest about the hours. A plan built on time you do not have produces failure and then calls it a discipline problem.

03

Five rules built into every plan

01

Work at the edge

Sessions aim for a success rate somewhere between half and most of the time. Getting everything right means you are performing rather than practising, so the difficulty goes up instead.

02

Reading is capped

Books, courses and videos are the most comfortable part of learning something and the least productive. Every plan limits how much of your week goes to taking things in, and pushes the rest into making something.

03

Gates, not weeks

Each phase ends with criteria someone else could check you against. You move on when you can do the thing, whatever the calendar says.

04

Feedback from outside your own head

Every skill here names where the honest signal lives: users, reviewers, buyers, a camera. Practise privately for long enough and you will plateau without noticing.

05

Lighter weeks are already in the plan

Anything over ten weeks includes reduced weeks. Taking the rest before you need it is what stops the whole thing falling over somewhere around week seven.

04

The daily dispatch

One passage a day from a tradition you pick: Stoicism, Taoism and Zen, strategy, mastery, meaning, or money and leverage. Each one comes with what it actually means, one thing to do about it today, and the argument against taking it too far. Most of these ideas get quoted far more often than they get used properly.
